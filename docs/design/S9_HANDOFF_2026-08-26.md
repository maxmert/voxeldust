### 3b. The stand-ins were still live, and on the crossing path

`RealmLevel::to_realm_id` still lowered a galaxy to `System(1)` — its own doc said "Slice 4 flips them",

### 3c. The cross-rung crossing had to be built — the largest piece, and unplanned

**I first ledgered this as P10 work, reasoning "nothing in the game does that yet". That was wrong.** A
dot leaving its home system for the galaxy IS a cross-rung crossing — the most ordinary journey in the
game. While S8's blanket guard stood, nothing could enter or leave the galaxy at all.

The fix is one sentence: **the arithmetic happens at the book's own rung.** A book's rows are placements
its anchor authored in the anchor's frame, so that is the unit they count in. The pose converts into it
on the way in and out into the destination's rung at the end.

- New primitive `Separation::convert_tier` — a difference needs its own, because it is routinely negative
  and not sub-cell, so the position converter would refuse it for pointing backwards.
- **Same-rung is the bit-for-bit identity**, measured over every rung × cell × residual case, so the
  shipped path pays nothing.
- **What still refuses, correctly:** a system cannot state its galaxy's centre in millimetres — 0.7 light
  years against a quarter-light-year reach. Pinned as a count: 4 of THE world's hops, exactly the two ring
  systems at both instants. The home system is the exception because it sits at the galaxy's origin.

### 3d. The 2048× trap hid in a dozen places — one of them writes the golden

A region's `center` is in its PARENT's frame; its `frame` is its own. A dozen readers assumed those were
the same. **`one_containment_answer` flattens each placement with the child's step** — left alone, this
slice would have blessed the galaxy's rows into the world's pinned reference bits 2048× wrong.

★ **The sharpest lesson of the slice:** when the ORACLE and the FIXTURE use the same wrong ruler they
agree with each other perfectly. The union-over-draw test looked correct right up until the real fold
disagreed with both.

## 6. ★ CLAIMS THAT ARE WRONG, AND MUST NOT BE RE-INHERITED

- **"The shipped placement caps the world at N = 4π = 12.57 systems"** and **"150,000 would be 28.07×
  inside the 1-light-year floor"** (s9_placement.md and the S9 climb run). **BOTH WRONG.** Mean spacing of
  N points on a sphere of radius R is `R·√(4π/N)`; at 2⁶² m with N = 150,000 that is **4.46 ly** —
  comfortably ABOVE the floor — and the cap at a 1-ly floor is ~674,000. The 4π figure comes from
  comparing spacing against the galaxy's own RADIUS instead of against the floor. **The spacing was never
  the problem. The SHAPE is.**
- **The residual bound.** `galaxy_sky.md` says 2⁻⁶³; the plan says 2⁻⁵¹; **I told the owner 2⁻⁵¹ and was
  wrong** (I used the binade above the value). The truth is PER RUNG: `step · ε / 4`. MEASURED tight:
  Fine→Galaxy 2⁻⁵³, Galaxy→Universe 2⁻³⁹.
- **"Crossing up is exact in the whole-number part"** (both the plan AND `galaxy_sky.md`). **FALSE** —
  `2047 × 2⁻¹⁰` plus the largest sub-cell offset rounds to exactly 2.0 and carries. **1,600 hits in a
  3,200-case directed sweep, 0 in 200,000 random draws.** A property test passes while the gate lies.
- **D-S8's published "0.098 % refinable"** is **0.0488 %** (I used ratio 1024 where it is 2048).

   named it in one line.
2. **A control that could fail is worth more than care.** The band fence "refused the world by 1.5e15 m"
   until a control asked the same question with today's numbers — the measurement was wrong, not the law.
3. **Coverage caught five faults in MY OWN tests**: an `&&` short-circuit, a lazily-evaluated failure
   message, an unreachable `panic!` arm, a `const fn` that only runs in the compiler, and an "exact"
   comparison built in floating point on BOTH sides (it measured zero error everywhere and passed).
4. **Asymmetric changes are the recurring bug of this slice** — three of them: the transport tag, the
   realm-token codec (writer without reader), and the centre unit. Each is a place where two sides must
   agree and NO TYPE ENFORCES IT. Look for the rest.

   nesting refusal named both bodies, both numbers, and refused to boot.
